# Reflection

## Decisions

One of the biggest decisions I made during this project was deciding how I wanted my portfolio to represent me. This is my personal developer portfolio website showcasing my work in web development and design. It highlights selected projects, shares a bit about me, and provides ways to connect, giving a quick overview of what I can build and how I approach my work.
I built this portfolio to create a professional online presence as I grow in my development career. It’s designed for employers, collaborators, and clients to explore my skills, view my work, and understand my design style, while also reflecting my progress and growth over time.
Because of that, I made the decision to keep the design clean, simple, and easy to navigate instead of trying to make it overly complicated. I wanted the projects and content to stand out more than flashy effects or overwhelming layouts. I also focused on making the site feel personal while still looking professional. Choosing colors, spacing, typography, and section layouts all came down to creating something that felt organized and approachable.
Another important decision was selecting which projects to include. I wanted to showcase work that reflected both my technical growth and my interest in thoughtful, user-centered design. Instead of adding everything I have worked on, I focused on projects that best represented my current abilities and goals.

## What Worked
One of the things that worked best during this project was the overall structure of the site. Breaking the portfolio into sections like About Me, Projects, Skills, and Contact helped keep the content organized and made the development process feel more manageable. I also think the visual consistency throughout the site worked well because it made everything feel connected instead of random.
I am most satisfied with the fact that I created something that genuinely represents me. Even though I am still learning and improving, this portfolio feels like proof of the progress I have made so far. Seeing my projects displayed in one place made me realize how much I have actually learned about web development and design.
I also feel more comfortable using HTML and CSS after completing this project. Styling the website and adjusting layouts taught me how important details are when building for the web. Small things like spacing, alignment, responsiveness, and readability made a much bigger difference than I originally expected.

## What I Would Do Differently
If I could start over, I would spend more time planning before jumping into development. A lot of my process involved making changes as I went, which sometimes made things feel disorganized or repetitive. Creating a clearer wireframe or layout plan from the beginning would have helped me save time and avoid unnecessary revisions later.
I would also spend more time improving responsiveness across different screen sizes. While the site works, I know there are areas that could feel more polished on mobile devices. Testing earlier and more consistently would have helped me catch layout issues sooner.
Another thing I would do differently is manage my GitHub workflow more carefully from the beginning. I ran into issues with repositories, pushing changes, and organizing versions of my work. Although it was frustrating at times, it taught me how important version control is during development.

## What I Learned
This project taught me that building for the web involves much more than just writing code. It requires planning, problem-solving, organization, and thinking about the user experience. I learned how HTML provides the structure of a website while CSS controls the visual presentation and overall feel of the page.
I also learned that development can be frustrating sometimes, especially when things do not work the way I expect them to. There were moments where I felt stuck trying to troubleshoot issues or understand why changes were not appearing correctly. However, I learned that persistence is a huge part of the process. A lot of development comes from experimenting, fixing mistakes, and continuing to learn through trial and error.
This project also helped me become more familiar with tools like GitHub, Cursor, and browser developer tools. I learned how important it is to stay organized, save progress consistently, and test changes often.
Most importantly, I learned that I am capable of building something meaningful from start to finish. This portfolio represents more than just a class project to me. It reflects my growth, creativity, and willingness to keep learning as I continue developing my skills in web development and design.
